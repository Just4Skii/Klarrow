## 1. Verified official source

**Canonical URL:** https://events.atmeta.com/aiacademysouthafrica

**Event name:** AI Academy South Africa Pitchathon (part of AI Academy South Africa, in partnership with DCDT).

Exact quotes from the official page:

> "The AI Academy South Africa is Meta's flagship capacity-building program designed to equip South African students, professionals, and startups with practical AI skills and tools."

> "The AI Academy South Africa Pitchathon is a dedicated track for early-stage South African startups with working AI prototypes or products built using Meta's AI technologies and open-source models. Ten selected startups will pitch their solutions for a chance to win:"

> "Applications close on October 20, 2026."

> "The top two pitchathon winners will each receive: US$5,000 in equity-free funding; Meta advertising credits; An invitation to attend Meta's Regional AI Summit in Istanbul, Türkiye, in November 2026."

Eligibility on the official page:

> "Be a startup or founding team based in South Africa"
> "Have a working prototype or MVP that demonstrates clear technical potential."
> "Have embedded at least one of Meta's AI technologies in their solution (For example, Llama, SAM, DINO, Muse, or PyTorch)."
> "Be solving a clearly defined problem with relevance to users, businesses, communities, or public institutions."
> "Be available to participate fully in all required Pitchathon activities, including an in-person pitch competition."

The page also says the selected startups pitch live in **Johannesburg**.

## 2. What this means for Klarrow

- The product's local `llama3.2:3b` model is a Meta Llama model and is used as part of the extraction pipeline. That is the strongest fit with the published "Meta AI technologies" example list.
- Ollama is the local runtime, not the model. Do not describe Ollama as a Meta technology; describe the model as Meta Llama 3.2 3B.
- The page does not explicitly confirm that local/offline Ollama deployment satisfies the requirement. The requirement is written broadly enough that it should, but that is an inference, not an organiser confirmation.
- The 19 October date in the working notes is an internal buffer. The official deadline is 20 October 2026. The page does not state a time zone.
- The page does not publish a judging rubric, pitch duration, deck/video limits, or the exact application questions.
- A 30 October pitch date appears in a secondary LinkedIn post, not in the official page text captured here. Treat it as unconfirmed.
