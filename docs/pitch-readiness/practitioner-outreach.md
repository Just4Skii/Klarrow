# Klarrow - Practitioner Outreach and 30-Day Validation Plan

**Status:** DRAFT ONLY. Nothing in this file has been sent, and no practitioner has been contacted or committed to a review. Do not describe this as a pilot, customer validation or an endorsement.

## 1. Purpose

We are testing whether Klarrow's evidence-reconciliation workflow resembles a real food-traceability investigation and where it would help or fail. We are seeking a 15-minute expert review of a synthetic demonstration, not a sales commitment.

## 2. Draft outreach message

> Subject: 15-minute review of a food-traceability investigation prototype?
>
> Hello [Name],
>
> I am building Klarrow, a human-in-the-loop workbench for food-traceability investigations. It is intended to help a QA operator reconcile fragmented supplier, receiving, production and dispatch records during a mock recall or supplier alert, while keeping source evidence, uncertainty and human decisions visible.
>
> I am not asking you to buy anything or to share confidential records. I would value 15 minutes of your experience: I will show a synthetic seven-document scenario and ask whether it resembles the real workflow, where it would help, and what would stop you trusting it.
>
> The prototype is early-stage. It does not make food-safety decisions, does not replace an ERP, and has not been validated on customer documents. If you are open to a short review, I can send three time options.
>
> Thank you,
> [Founder]
> Klarrow

## 3. Five review questions

1. Does this resemble a real investigation workflow, and where does it diverge?
2. Which step, if any, would be most useful in a real mock recall or supplier alert?
3. What would stop you trusting or adopting it?
4. Which records, systems or integrations would be necessary for it to be useful?
5. What would need to be proven before you would consider a supervised pilot?

## 4. 30-day validation plan

**Scope:** one practitioner-led, permission-based mock-recall exercise using authorised or redacted records. This is a plan, not completed research.

### Week 1 - Frame and recruit
- Identify 3-5 food-safety/quality practitioners at processors, exporters or consultancies.
- Ask for a 15-minute review of the synthetic scenario.
- Record only consented, anonymised notes.
- Do not call these conversations "customer validation" unless the practitioner explicitly agrees to a pilot.

### Week 2 - Expert review
- Walk through the Run 4 scenario and the honest limitations.
- Capture: workflow fit, missing records, trust blockers, integration needs, review burden.
- Ask for one redacted or synthetic document set that resembles their real records.

### Week 3 - Timed tabletop
- Define a locked ground truth with the practitioner.
- Run one manual baseline and one Klarrow-assisted pass on equivalent synthetic/redacted documents.
- Measure: time to assemble an evidence pack, missed or ambiguous links, human review load, unresolved gaps, and whether the participant can explain the confirmed/possible distinction.
- Preserve the artifacts and note any disagreement.

### Week 4 - Decide
- Write a short validation memo: what worked, what failed, what must change.
- Decide whether to seek a supervised pilot or return to product discovery.
- Do not claim accuracy, time savings or demand unless the exercise actually measured them.

## 5. Evidence discipline

- No invented interviews, quotes, LOIs, pilots or willingness-to-pay figures.
- No customer documents without explicit permission and a data-handling agreement.
- The synthetic Run 4 benchmark remains a functional test, not validation.
- If a practitioner declines or cannot participate, record that as an open gap.
