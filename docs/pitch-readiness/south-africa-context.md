# Klarrow - South African Context and Competitive Notes

**Status:** Research notes. Not customer validation. Access date 2026-10-18.

## 1. Why a South African framing is plausible

- **Producer responsibility, local enforcement.** National Department of Health, Food Control: "The primary responsibility for food safety and accurate labelling rests with the producer, manufacturer, seller or importer." Enforcement is delegated to municipal health services. https://www.health.gov.za/food-control-home/
- **Export audits explicitly cover traceability.** PPECB Food Safety Services says its audits focus on "worker hygiene, use of plant protection products and traceability together with good agricultural practices" for regulated agricultural food products intended for export. https://ppecb.com/services/food-safety-services/
- **Regulator can require an investigation or recall.** Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008, s60: the NCC may require a producer to "conduct an investigation" or "carry out a recall programme on any terms required by the Commission." https://www.gov.za/documents/consumer-protection-act
- **Real SA outbreak example.** NICD's 2018 listeriosis statement describes reconciling clinical isolates, patient interviews, environmental samples and final product samples. https://www.nicd.ac.za/statement-regarding-the-source-of-the-listeriosis-outbreak/

These sources support a problem narrative about multi-source evidence reconciliation. They do **not** prove that a specific buyer has this pain, will pay, or lacks an existing tool.

## 2. Claims to avoid

- Do not say FSMA 204 applies in South Africa. It is a US rule. It may matter to SA exporters selling into the US, but say that explicitly.
- Do not invent SA recall statistics, mandatory mock-recall times, or "most factories use spreadsheets".
- Do not say R638/R146/R723 requires a specific lot-level trace or recall deadline; those regulations were not text-verified here.
- Do not say PPECB certifies all food exports; its scope is perishable produce.
- Do not say ISO 22000/BRCGS/FSSC 22000 is legally mandatory in South Africa.
- Do not say Klarrow is first, best, cheapest, only, or superior without direct evidence.

## 3. Neutral competitor positioning

| Vendor / product | Own positioning | Where traceability sits | What is not established on the public page |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aptean Food & Beverage | Industry ERP/PLM on Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central | Bidirectional lot tracking and recall readiness | No SA regulatory mapping; no independent comparison |
| Foods Connected | End-to-end supply-chain, food safety, compliance and traceability | Track & Trace / supplier collaboration | No SA regulatory mapping; one US FSMA 204 testimonial |
| Ideagen Safefood 360° | Food safety, supplier quality and compliance management | Recalls inside a broader management system | No SA-specific regulatory content |
| SYSPRO | ERP for manufacturing and distribution | Lot traceability in ERP modules | No SA regulatory mapping on the reviewed page |

Neutral reading: these are different centres of gravity (ERP, FSMS, supplier collaboration). None of the reviewed pages positions itself specifically as a South African recall-evidence reconciliation layer. That is a hypothesis to test, not a proven gap.

## 4. Assumptions for practitioner validation

1. Lot genealogy is split across ERP/MES, QA releases, COAs, packing records, spreadsheets or paper.
2. Mock recalls are recurring and measured, with time-to-trace or mass balance as a KPI.
3. Different audiences (municipal health, PPECB/SA GAP, NCC/DoH, retail customers) demand different evidence packs.
4. Contradictory evidence is the hard part, not document storage.
5. Buyers will adopt an overlay beside existing ERP/FSMS rather than replace it.
